# MET-103: A disk of a Btrfs volume is missing

A disk of a Btrfs volume is missing or has failed. The message names the volume by its label, or its UUID, and says how many of its disks are missing out of how many it has. A volume that keeps a second copy of its data still mounts and works, from the copies on the other disks, but until the disk is replaced it has no second copy, and the failure of one more disk loses data. The warning is critical.

## What to do

1. Run `log -k` to see what the kernel said when the disk went, and check the disk's cable and power. If it comes back, the volume mounts with it again, and it is behind until a scrub catches it up (MET-104).
2. If the disk is gone for good, put a new one in its place with `replace start`. A missing disk is named by its device ID, its place in the volume: 2 for the second disk. For the volume mounted at /volumes/vda, whose second disk is replaced by /devices/vdc:

   ```
   replace start 2 /devices/vdc /volumes/vda
   ```

   It rebuilds what the old disk held onto the new one from the copies on the others, and returns when it is done. The volume stays in use meanwhile.
3. Check that it worked: `metrics btrfs.missing` shows 0 for the volume, and the warning is cleared.

A volume with no second copy of its data, one disk with no RAID, has nothing to rebuild from: `replace` refuses it, and the data that was on the missing disk is lost.
