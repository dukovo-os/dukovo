# HW-123: A processor got too hot under load

While every CPU ran at full load, the check read the processor's temperature every 2 seconds, and judged the highest of each sensor by the limits its hardware monitor gives. A failure means a sensor reached its critical limit, at which the processor slows itself down hard or turns the machine off to save itself. A warning means it came within 5 °C of its high limit.

## What to do

1. Check that the fans turn. Run `sensors` to see their speeds and the temperatures, and look inside for a fan that is still.
2. Clear the vents and the heatsink fins of dust, with the machine off. A laptop's vents are under it and at its back; keep them off a bed or a lap.
3. Check that the cooler is seated and tight, and that the thermal paste is not dry. A cooler that was moved or an old paste gives temperatures that rise fast under load.
4. Check that the room is not hot, and that the machine's case has air coming in and going out.
5. To check that it worked, run `elevate hardware check cpu` again and see the temperature line pass.
