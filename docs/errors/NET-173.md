# NET-173: The update server's certificate is not trusted

With `--internet`, something answered for the update server, but its certificate is signed by an authority this system does not trust, or is for another name. The system checks it against its list of root certificates, which /system/settings/ssl holds.

On a network of a hotel, a company or a school, this is usually a proxy or a sign-in page answering in the server's place, or a firewall that opens TLS connections and signs its own with an authority of the network's.

## What to do

1. Sign in to the network's page, if it has one: a browser on another machine shows it.
2. If the network's authority is one you trust, add its certificate to the system's, as dukovo-certificates(7) says.
3. If the server is your own, check that its certificate was issued for the name in the `server` setting of /system/settings/sysupdate/updates.toml, and that its chain is complete.
4. Otherwise do not trust it: use another network.
5. Run `netcheck --internet` again.

## See also

netcheck(8), dukovo-certificates(7), updates.toml(5), the handbook's chapters "Running services: web, databases and certificates" and "Checking the network".
