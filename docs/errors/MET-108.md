# MET-108: A disk is close to its limit

A disk has been at or over its warning temperature for 5 readings in a row. diskhealth reads each disk's temperature every ten minutes, and metricsd keeps it as the series `temp{disk/NAME}`. A disk that is asleep is never woken for it, so its last reading stays until it wakes. The message names the disk and its model and says its temperature and its limit. The limit is the one the disk says itself, or, for a disk that says none, the one for its kind: 50 °C and 60 °C for a hard disk, 65 °C and 70 °C for a solid-state disk, and 70 °C and 80 °C for an NVMe drive. The warning is critical at the second of them. It is cleared when the disk is 3 degrees below the line it was raised at.

A disk that is kept hot wears out sooner, and a hard disk that gets too hot can lose data.

## What to do

1. Run `diskhealth NAME` to see the disk's record, and `hardware sensors` to see every temperature the machine reads.
2. Check that the fans turn, that the air can reach the disks, and that the disks are not packed against each other or against a warm part. A disk in a closed cupboard needs more air.
3. Spread the load: a scrub, a backup or a check of the whole disk keeps it busy for hours, and `metrics show 'temp{disk/NAME}' --since 1d` shows when it was hottest.
4. To check that it worked, run `metrics show 'temp{disk/NAME}' --since 1h`: the temperature must fall. The warning is cleared within an hour of it falling.
