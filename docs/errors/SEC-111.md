# SEC-111: Any account may sign in over SSH with its password

SSH is on, passwords sign in, and `/system/settings/ssh/sshd_config` names neither `AllowUsers` nor `AllowGroups`. So the password of every person's account is one to guess, the accounts nobody uses among them, such as one made for a guest and forgotten, whose passwords are the weakest. Root never signs in over SSH, and an empty password never does. Naming who may sign in cuts the accounts that can be guessed to the few that need it.

## What to do

1. Decide who needs to sign in over SSH. For the people who run the computer, the group `admins` does: put `AllowGroups admins` in `/system/settings/ssh/sshd_config`. For named people, put `AllowUsers alice bob`. The names are patterns, and the lists apply to keys as well as passwords.
2. Run `sshd -t`, which checks the file, then `elevate service sshd reload`. A sign-in from someone who is not named is refused from then on, so keep a session open until another one signs in.
3. Better, turn passwords off, as SEC-110 says: with keys alone, each account lists its own keys, and this line passes.
4. To check that it worked, run `elevate dukovo-check`: the line for `who may sign in` passes, and names who may.
